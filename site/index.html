Rustle

The sound of leaves. The noise the order book makes before it shows up on a candle.

Not investment advice.

Rustle is, today, a research tool. It makes no recommendations and no performance claims, and its signals are unvalidated by design — measuring whether they have any predictive power at all is the current work. Provided “AS IS”, without warranties of any kind.

The goal

An automated real-time trading system for Upbit — reading the order book as it moves, deciding on its own, executing without a human in the loop.

Getting there honestly means earning the right to trade first, so it is being built bottom-up. Live execution is the destination; a signal that survives out-of-sample validation is the ticket.

What it does today

Records public Upbit trade and orderbook streams to local Parquet files, detects microstructure signals in them, and measures whether those signals predicted anything after the fact.

Method

  • Explainable rules, not a model. Every signal carries an evidence field naming what triggered it. A signal without evidence cannot be published.
  • Selection and validation are separate. Days 1–14 pick candidate rules; days 15–28 are touched only to validate them. 28 contiguous UTC days are required.
  • A rule has to clear a bar. At least 50 validation signals and a wholly-positive paired-bootstrap confidence interval.
  • Execution comes last, on purpose. No credentials and no order submission until a signal is validated. Simulated fills are the only “execution” today, and they ignore slippage, partial fills, and fees — so paper results are an upper bound, not a forecast.

Roadmap

  1. Collect — continuous capture of public Upbit streams. Working.
  2. Detect — explainable microstructure rules. Working.
  3. Validate — out-of-sample proof against a random baseline. Current.
  4. Execute (paper) — real-time decision loop, simulated fills. Partial.
  5. Execute (live) — order submission behind risk limits and a kill switch. Gated on step 3.

Status

Step 3 of 5, at the kill gate: if detected signals show no edge over a random baseline, the signal definitions change or the project stops. An automated trader built on an unvalidated signal is an expensive way to pay fees, so steps 4 and 5 stay closed until this one clears.
